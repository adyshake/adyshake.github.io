Eat too much, get fat. Smoke too much, get cancer. Drink too much, get liver damage. Read too much news, anxiety. Phone ping too much, anxiety.

Moderation is a key aspect of life that would serve us well in nearly every domain. Yet, in our digital lives, we mostly accept the defaults and live with it. Do you really need to be interrupted with buzz for every text message you receive, or do you accept the reality that most things can wait in life. I understand that this is a privileged position to have, but if you have the privilege, I think it makes sense that you use it to better your life in someway.

Digital moderation is something I have tried to inculcate and failed many times, but I think I may have finally figured it out??? Mandatory disclaimer: I might be wrong (like I am about a lot of things in life).

# Turn your notifications off

Yes, really. Turn it off for almost every single app except for ones that you do require. For me, that's just phone calls and the calendar. My WhatsApp, iMessage and basically everything else is off. I understand the need to be alerted for DMs sometimes, for that I use Signal, which has notifications on. But my folks know that messaging me there is an active interrupt.

I've lived this way now for about a year and you'll be really surprised that most things in life don't require immediate attention. If someone really wants to reach out, trust me, they'll call you or page you at work.

A nice side effect of this that, you'll tend to stick with tasks or whatever you're doing for longer, and will only reach for your phone if you have a moment in between. You get to choose when to switch your focus. Similarly, my wife's done the same thing and it's so much easier to remain present in the moment and talk to each other without being distracted by buzzing notifications.

# No lock screen notifications

This is a follow up to the previous section. If you intend to use your phone more intentionally, this will help you again. You will pick your phone most often with a specific goal in mind -- whether that's checking the weather, adding a reminder, looking for a photo -- and if you've got lock screen notifications, it will take you away a little bit from whatever you set out to do and might derail you as well.

I usually just keep phone calls, reminders and some slack notifications (that don't buzz me) here during work hours.

# Disable algorithmic recommendations

In my humble opinion, so many algorithmic generated feeds are usually just really well disguised ads. Someone is always trying to sell you something, either directly or indirectly. This is especially true for platforms like Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn, less so for music streaming like Spotify et al.

Facebook and Instagram are the obvious ones. But why YouTube as well? I've found that I can't really stop consuming good content there and tend to lose most of my evenings. Not having any feeds induces quite a lot of boredom, which forces me to just do other things that I normally wouldn't do if there were cheaper, easier ways to kill time.

Spotify is also an interesting case for me. As a service, I quite enjoy it. But having unlimited music always meant that I would never stick with an entire album and once again, consume what's recommended to me by algorithmic feeds. In this case, I swapped out one feed for another, though more human, through Radio Paradise. I discover albums through there and listen to music offline through Amperfy.

# Closing thoughts
I've considered dumb phones as a replacement for my smartphone. But if you've ever tried it, it's quite impossible to function in a society that expects you have a charged internet-connected supercomputer in your pocket at all times. Society has quietly reorganized itself around this expectation and while it's annoying, it's hard to escape this reality. The interventions I've described above does more than enough to achieve roughly the same benefit you'd have with a dumbphone without having to endure daily sacrifices in other areas of your life.

It's all about moderation.
